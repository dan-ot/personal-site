# About

A quick note about the site. I definitely used AI in the development work (you'll see that as I publish technical blogs). I used it to help research what framework and platform and how to deploy them. I do not use it to help write the content - it's important to me that every word is my thought, and there is no way to save labor on that.

## Personal

The short version? I'm a homeschooled, self-taught software engineer. I carry my critical thinking and passion for quality and results into every endeavor. I do my best to base my thinking and my work off of first principles, so my results stand the test of time yet satisfy their current context.

I moved a lot growing up. My parents also sought out friends of different backgrounds and experiences, so I had a wide variety of exposure throughout my childhood. I've lived under a variety of regulatory environments, in a variety of climates, eating a large variety of foods.

I think honesty is critical for a healthy life and a successful community or organization. You must be honest with yourself and with each other to identify the actual problems you face and the resources you have. Once you have identified the problems, you must attack them - problems only get worse over time.

You must also have clarity of purpose or a strong vision. With such clarity, the decision about which problems should be solved first becomes much more clear. Aligning decisions with a vision also provides insight on how to decide which problems are worth the resources to address.

Finally, the human superpower is teamwork - having diverse experiences and thoughts aimed at solving the same problem. I find most capable people (and most people are capable) will come to a close enough agreement if they start from the same place - a clear vision, understanding of the problem and resources, and the ability to express and work out any differences between them. To dig in, there is no star player important enough to sacrifice the health of a performing team.

## Philosophical

As I may write in the blog at some point, labels are both natural and dangerous. Some people live by them, so I'll give the ones I gladly apply to myself. There is a trap in supplying labels - an implication that the labels will define my behavior. It might be good practice to avoid exposing these labels

We either know each other, in which case you will likely not be surprised by these, or we don't, in which case you'll either fall into the trap and react to me based on my classifications or you will avoid the trap and react to me based on my behavior. If we don't know each other, I'd prefer to know whether you will fall into that trap or avoid early.

### Christian
So, the first and primary label for me is Christian. I came to my Christianity outside of a church environment, so I don't really have a further label than that. It's also a subculture where people tend to apply a label and dig in on the differences instead of the core things that should help them form a community, so I'm not particularly interested in refining that.

### Libertarian
Probably the label you will hear me bring up the most is Libertarian. This is both a political and an economic position. It comes from personal experience and extensive reading, along with observation bridging the two. The core of my personal approach is that it is wrong to apply force to other people - persuasion is more efficient, more effective, and more moral. In addition, power both corrupts and attracts the corrupt - an optimal system will have the least amount of power collected together as possible, and pit necessary powers against each other to help balance them.
